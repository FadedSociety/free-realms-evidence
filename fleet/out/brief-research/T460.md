**Client evidence (15/4):**
- Reader `0x00b0f680`. Header via `0x00b0f550`, which reads two u16 values (stored at +4/+8). The caller presumably checks 15 and 4 (R).
- Stream order as decompiled:
  1. u64 at struct +0x10 (speaker)
  2. i32 at +0x18 (string id)
  3. three bytes at +0x1c, +0x1d, +0x1e. **Each is stored as `!= 0` (bool)**, so they read as flags, not raw u8.
  4. i32 at +0x20 (colour)
  5. u64 at **+0x30** (first 8-byte read after colour)
  6. u64 at **+0x28** (second)
  7. i32 at +0x38
- Total 4+8+4+3+4+8+8+4 = 43 bytes, matching the brief. The brief's "x, y" names are (R); the stream bytes are what the reader proves.
- Dispatcher `0x00b14350`:
  - WorldSay (`0x00b0fad0` -> `0x009012b0`) is called at `0x00b14e17` when one dispatcher-local byte (`cStack_404`) is 0.
  - Emote (`0x00b0e190` -> `0x00901a50`) is called at `0x00b14dec` in the else branch, when a different byte (`uStack_403>>8`) is 0.
  - The brief's "branch at `0x00b14db5`" was not seen. Its "a != 0 goes to emote" is not what the pseudocode shows. The mapping of these two bytes to wire a/b/c is unverified; a golden test should follow the reader, not the brief's routing claim.
